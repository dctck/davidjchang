The Problem

The Challenge

Beam already let creators distribute digital collectibles through QR codes, but every campaign still depended on our team. Creators submitted a request, waited for us to configure it, and could miss the moment they were trying to support.

Making Beam self-service meant creators also had to take on the knowledge and safeguards our internal team had been providing. The existing tool was too technical for that, and automated claims could drain campaigns before the intended community had a chance to participate.

User Pain Points

"The approval process takes too long. My event will be over by then."

"The interface is too technical. I don't understand these settings."

"Bots grabbed everything before my real fans could claim anything."

Research & Discovery

Who Creators Were Actually Trying to Reach

Early creator interviews showed that most campaigns were not broad giveaways. They were designed for communities creators already had:

Digital Campaigns

Key Insight

Beam was not just a giveaway tool. It was a community tool, so distribution only worked if the intended people actually received the items.

The Bot Problem

Early campaigns exposed a second problem: automated claims could sweep available items within minutes, leaving real supporters with nothing to claim.

What self-service had to protect:

Prevent mass automated claiming
Let creators control who could participate
Make campaign status and distribution visible

Solution Design

Designing the Self-Service Experience

Because there were few direct blockchain examples, I looked at adjacent tools such as QR generators, campaign managers, and ticketing products. The strongest pattern was progressive disclosure: guide people through only the decisions they need at each step.

A Complete Creator Workflow

The final product covered the full creator journey: create a Beam, choose how it would be distributed, protect access, monitor claims, and manage active campaigns.

Recipient Claim Experience

A visual, shareable claim experience designed around the moment someone receives an item

Creator Protection

Optional safeguards let creators protect campaigns without exposing technical complexity

Campaign Analytics

Track distribution, claim activity, and campaign progress

Campaign Management

A scannable dashboard shows campaign status and progress at a glance

Protecting the Intended Audience

The goal was not to expose every security mechanism. It was to give creators understandable ways to control access when their campaign needed it.

Access Controls

Community Access

Limit participation to known communities or verified groups

Region Controls

Restrict access in regions associated with abuse

Holder Access

Limit claims to holders of a specific asset

Per-person Limits

Reduce repeat claims with wallet-based limits

Distribution Options

One multi-use QR: One shareable code for social or community distribution
Many single-use QRs: Individual codes for events or controlled distribution
Flexible controls: Add access restrictions only when a campaign needs them

Designing for the Recipient

The creator side is a tool. The claim side is the moment someone receives something.

Creator branding and campaign messaging
Clear item preview and availability
Simple claiming guidance
A visual experience designed to be shared

Why I’m Proud of This Project

We Removed Ourselves From the Workflow

Creators no longer needed our team to configure every campaign, turning an operational bottleneck into a product they could use on their own.

Protection Supported the Core Experience

The goal was not simply to distribute more items. It was to make sure creators could reach the communities they actually intended to reward.

One System, Two Different Experiences

The creator side prioritized clarity, status, and control. The recipient side was deliberately more visual and immediate because receiving the item was the experience.

What I Learned

Self-Service Has to Replace Staff Knowledge

Removing staff from a workflow only works when the product also replaces the guidance and safeguards they used to provide.

Security Can Enable the Experience

Protection mattered because a campaign failed if the intended community never got a chance to participate.

Design Around the Real Audience

Most creators were distributing to communities they already had, which changed how we thought about access and control.

Reveal Complexity When It Is Needed

Keep the common path simple, then expose advanced controls only when a campaign requires them.
