The Problem

Users had to navigate wallets, networks, and addresses before they could do something as simple as Send.

What Users Were Struggling With

"It takes too many steps just to send ENJ."

"Switching between multiple wallets and addresses is confusing."

"Why do I have to dig into each coin just to send or receive?"

The Product Was Organized Around the System

The old wallet mirrored the underlying technical architecture:

Wallets (collapsible groups)
Addresses (grouped by networks like ETH, BTC)
Asset details
Only then: Send or Receive

The structure made sense technically, but users had to understand the system before they could complete a basic task. I wanted to reverse that relationship: start with intent, then reveal the technical details only when needed.

Different Users, Same Friction

Power users, newer users, and game developers had different needs, but all three groups were slowed down by the same problem: too much structure before a simple action.

Power Users

Multiple wallets for different purposes, trades frequently

New Users

Solution Design

Make Intent the Entry Point

Instead of making users enter through a wallet or network, I introduced an action-first entry point. Send and Receive addressed the core user need, while Stake and Mint used the same high-visibility area to improve discovery of strategic features.

UX SOLUTION

Designing the Send Flow Around Asset Intent

Challenge

Once users chose Send, what should they decide first?

Asset first, technical details second

  1. 1. Choose the asset they want to send
  2. 2. Choose from compatible source addresses
  3. 3. Choose the destination and amount

Users thought in terms of the asset they wanted to move, not the network architecture behind it. Selecting the asset first let the product narrow the compatible addresses automatically and turn a technical search into a guided decision.

Keep Complex Wallet Management Available, Not in the Way

New Wallet Selector

A global wallet selector gave power users fast access to multiple wallets without forcing newer users through that complexity.

User Need and Business Goals Shared the Same Surface

The same high-frequency area that simplified Send and Receive also improved visibility for Stake and Mint.

What I Learned

Intent Can Matter More Than Hierarchy

A technically accurate hierarchy is not always the clearest mental model for users. For frequent tasks, starting with intent can be more useful than exposing the system structure first.

UX and Business Goals Do Not Have to Compete

When strategic features are placed around genuine user behavior, better usability and better product discovery can reinforce each other.
